FIDELITY INDEX · 02 — HOW CLOSE IS “CLOSE”?

How close is “close”?

Scientists grade an analogue on four things. A site can be a near-perfect match on one and a poor one on another.

F.01

Landform

Dunes, craters, lava tubes and polygons that match the shapes found on another world.

F.02

Chemistry

Acidic brines, iron oxides and sulphates that echo the minerals found on Mars.

F.03

Life

Extremophiles and stromatolites that hint at how life could cling on elsewhere.

F.04

Operations

Isolation, communication delay and suit work that rehearse a real mission.

⚠ Reality check

What Earth always gives away

Every analogue shares one problem: it is still Earth. These four differences are what make the real thing so hard.

  • Air A thick nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere that Mars and the Moon do not have.
  • Water Liquid water, ice and rain, even in the driest deserts.
  • Gravity 1 g here, versus 0.38 g on Mars and 0.17 g on the Moon.
  • Life Microbes, lichens and roots that colonise even extreme sites.
03

RED ZONE · 03 — MARS ON EARTH

Mars, already on Earth.

Six places where the ground, the chemistry or the isolation rehearses the Red Planet.

◉ Mars analogue 22.9°S · 68.2°W
ChileHyperarid · Rover testing

Atacama Desert

The driest non-polar desert on Earth. Instruments for Viking, Phoenix and ExoMars were tested in its Mars-like soils.

Reality check Still has an oxygen atmosphere and rare rain.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 2

◉ Mars analogue 77.5°S · 162.3°E
AntarcticaCold desert · Astrobiology

McMurdo Dry Valleys

Snow-free valleys that may not have seen rain for millions of years. Blood Falls stains the ice red with iron-rich brine.

Reality check Summer meltwater and an oxygen atmosphere.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 1

◉ Mars analogue 75.4°N · 89.7°W
CanadaImpact · Isolation

Haughton Crater, Devon Island

A 23 km impact structure in a polar desert. NASA’s Haughton-Mars Project tests habitats and communication delay here.

Reality check Earth gravity and slow polar weathering.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 10

◉ Mars analogue 64.9°N · 19.0°W
IcelandVolcanic · Rover testing

Holuhraun & Krafla

Fissure-fed lava fields and altered basalt. Apollo crews trained here, and a Mars 2020 rover prototype was tested in 2019.

Reality check Active ice and water, and an atmosphere.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 12

◉ Mars analogue 29.0°N · 13.6°W
SpainVolcanic · Lava tubes

Timanfaya, Lanzarote

Pristine 18th-century lava and tubes. ESA’s Pangaea course trains astronauts in field geology on this island.

Reality check An atmosphere and vegetation nearby.

ESA Pangaea · Planetary Analogue Catalogue

◉ Mars analogue 37.7°N · 6.6°W
SpainMineralogical · Astrobiology

Río Tinto

An acidic, iron-rich river precipitating jarosite and hematite, minerals also found at Meridiani Planum on Mars.

Reality check The acidity comes from pyrite mining runoff, not Martian groundwater.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 23

Also used Dallol, Ethiopia Svalbard, Norway Tenerife, Spain Pilbara, Australia Namib, Namibia Utah, USA Boulby, UK
04

GREY ZONE · 04 — MOON ON EARTH

Moon, rehearsed on Earth.

Grey lava, cinder and impact craters, the ground astronauts walked before they ever left orbit. ⟵ drag sideways ⟶

◐ Moon analogue 43.4°N · 113.5°W
USAVolcanic · Apollo training

Craters of the Moon, Idaho

A sea of young basalt. Apollo 14 astronauts trained here to read volcanic terrain before walking on the Moon.

Reality check Earth gravity and a thin atmosphere.

National Park Service · mission history

◐ Moon analogue 35.3°N · 111.5°W
USAImpact · Apollo training

Cinder Lake Crater Field

A crater field built in the 1960s from a Lunar Orbiter image, so Apollo crews could practise on the ground.

Reality check Man-made, and far smaller than lunar crater fields.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 7

◐ Moon analogue 35.0°N · 111.0°W
USAImpact · Geology training

Meteor Crater, Arizona

A 1.2 km bowl roughly 50,000 years old. Astronauts studied impact geology here before the Apollo missions.

Reality check Earth gravity and weathering.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 16

◐ Moon analogue 19.8°N · 155.5°W
USAVolcanic · Instrument testing

Mauna Kea, Hawaii

Apollo astronauts trained on these cinder slopes. NASA later tested lunar instruments and ISRU equipment here.

Reality check An atmosphere, water, and cultural access limits.

Planetary Analogue Catalogue

◐ Moon analogue 65.0°N · 16.7°W
IcelandVolcanic · Apollo training

Askja & Lambahraun

Black sands and craters where Apollo 11 and 12 crews learned field geology in the 1960s.

Reality check Ice, water and an atmosphere.

ESA Catalogue · ch. 12

◐ Moon analogue 41.7°N · 121.5°W
USALava tubes · Modelling

Lava Beds National Monument

Basaltic tubes and skylights used to model how lunar lava tubes could be mapped and eventually built in.

Reality check Terrestrial tubes are about 30 m wide, far smaller than lunar ones.

Planetary Analogue Catalogue

Also used Lanzarote, Spain Haleakala, Hawaii Pisgah Crater, California Lonar Crater, India Mistastin Lake, Canada Sudbury, Canada
